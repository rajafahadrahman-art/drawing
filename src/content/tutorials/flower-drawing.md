intro: >-
  A simple bloom is one of the friendliest ways to practise overlapping curves.
  In this flower drawing lesson you will block in a center, arrange petals
  around it, and keep the stem and leaves from competing with the blossom. The
  difficulty stays beginner-friendly because the construction uses ovals and
  arcs rather than complicated anatomy. By the end you should have a clean
  blossom with readable petal overlaps and a steady stem. Work lightly at first
  so you can adjust spacing before committing to darker contours.
beforeYouStart: >-
  Set the page so there is room for the full open bloom with a light stem curve.
  Use an HB pencil for the first pass, keep the pressure pale, and mark the
  largest direction lines before drawing petal veins and leaf edges. A small
  scrap sheet is useful for testing curves and shadows.
refine: >-
  Refine the flower by comparing the outer silhouette against the inner
  landmarks. Clean the construction lines that cross petals and stem, then
  strengthen only the edges that describe overlap, weight, or the main focal
  area.
shading: >-
  Shade lightly from one direction so the petals, stem, and leaves share the
  same light source. Deepen small contact shadows and leave highlights open on
  the most forward forms.
beginnerVariation: >-
  For an easy simple version, skip the smallest texture marks and draw a flower
  with only the main round center, soft petal ring, and gently bending stem. Use
  one clean outline, one shadow shape, and no background details.
detailedVariation: >-
  For a more detailed study, add secondary overlaps, vary the line weight around
  the open bloom with a light stem curve, and spend extra time on alternate
  petal lengths around the center so the blossom does not look stamped. Keep the
  added marks lighter than the main contour.
commonMistakes:

    Starting the flower with final dark outlines before the round center, soft

  - >-

  - >-

  - >-
    Shading every area evenly instead of separating the light side from the

steps:
  - title: Place the main construction shapes
    body: >-
      Sketch the first structure with pale lines: round center, soft petal ring,
      and gently bending stem. Keep the marks loose and look at the whole page
      rather than one detail. This is the only place where the full flower
      drawing phrase needs attention; after that, the drawing can grow from
      landmarks. Leave enough margin around the open bloom with a light stem
      curve so later refinements do not feel cramped.
    tip: Use the side of the pencil for soft construction lines.
  - title: Block in the petals
    body: >-
      Add the petals using simple curves that follow the first shape. Compare
      their size to the main body before adding detail. If the spacing feels
      uneven, redraw the guide rather than forcing the final outline. Lightly
      mark where the stem will sit so the parts relate to each other and the
      silhouette stays readable.
    tip: Check the largest spacing before erasing any guide lines.
  - title: Set the stem and leaves
    body: >-
      Place the stem next, then attach the leaves with a clean overlap. Watch
      for tangents where two edges only touch; a small overlap usually looks
      more natural. Keep the new lines lighter than the main contour. The goal
      is to show how the features connect to the form, not to finish every small
      texture mark yet.
    tip: Overlap forms clearly so each part feels attached.
  - title: Refine the outside contour
    body: >-
      Trace around the outer edge slowly and turn the basic shapes into a more
      specific contour. Use longer strokes on calm areas and shorter strokes
      where the form changes direction. Adjust the open bloom with a light stem
      curve before adding texture. If one side feels too heavy, compare the
      empty space around it and shave the line back with light erasing.
    tip: Darken only the contour you are sure about.
  - title: Add subject details
    body: >-
      Work on petal veins and leaf edges with small marks that follow the
      surface. Keep the details grouped instead of spreading identical marks
      everywhere. Add a few accents near the focal area, then leave quieter
      spaces so the drawing can breathe. The center disk and bud should support
      the structure rather than distract from the main shape.
    tip: Cluster detail near the focal point and simplify the edges.
  - title: Clean the guide lines
    body: >-
      Erase construction lines that cut through finished features, especially
      around the petals and leaves. Do not scrub the paper; lift graphite slowly
      and redraw any softened edges afterward. This cleanup stage is also a good
      time to correct small proportion issues. Step back from the page and check
      whether the subject still reads clearly at a glance.
    tip: Use a kneaded eraser if the paper surface is delicate.
  - title: Add light shading and finish
    body: >-
      Choose one light direction and place gentle shadows where forms overlap or
      turn away. Add a cast shadow only if it helps ground the flower in the
      garden sketch page. For this flower drawing, keep highlights open and
      avoid covering the whole sketch with gray. Finish by strengthening the
      most important contour lines and softening any leftover construction
      marks.
    tip: One consistent light source is better than many scattered shadows.

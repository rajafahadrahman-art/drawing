  - question: What is the easiest way to start a Hello Kitty drawing?

    answer: 'Her bow sits on her left ear (the right side as you look at her), tilted slightly outward. Draw two rounded loops with a circle in the middle, overlapping the ear and the head outline.'
  - question: Does Hello Kitty have a mouth?
    answer: 'No — Hello Kitty is drawn without a mouth, which is part of her design. If your drawing looks slightly off, check whether you added one by habit.'
  - question: How do I draw Hello Kitty and friends together?
    answer: 'Use the same steps for each character and change the ears and accessories: round ears and a scarf for a bear friend, long ears for a bunny. Keep heads the same size and line the friends up on one ground line.'
  - question: What colours do I need for Hello Kitty?
    answer: 'White for her head and body, pink or red for the bow and dress, yellow for the nose, and black for the eyes, whiskers, and outlines. That small palette covers the classic look.'

beforeYouStart: 'Hello Kitty is all about placement: her eyes sit low and wide, her nose is a small yellow oval between them, and the bow always sits on one ear. Use an HB pencil, draw the head oval big and pale, and mark light guides for the eye line before adding any features.'
refine: 'Refine by checking the face spacing: eyes level with each other, nose centred between and just below them, whiskers fanning evenly on both cheeks. Soften the head outline into one smooth curve, clean the guides, and keep the bow crisp — it is the first thing fans look for.'
shading: 'Hello Kitty is a flat-colour character, so skip heavy shading. A touch of soft pink on the cheeks is plenty. Clean, even colour inside bold outlines is what makes a hello kitty easy drawing look right.'
beginnerVariation: 'For the simplest hello kitty easy drawing, draw just the head: oval, ears, bow, two dot eyes, yellow nose, and six whiskers. It reads instantly and fits on a card, a notebook, or a lunchbox note.'

conclusion: 'Hello Kitty rewards careful placement over complicated lines: a wide oval, a tidy bow, and a calm little face. Draw the head-only version first, then add the dress body once the face feels right every time.'

steps:
  - title: Draw the Head Oval
    body: 'Sketch a wide, gently flattened oval for the head — broader than it is tall, with a slightly flatter bottom where the chin would be. Draw it big and pale, and add a faint horizontal guide low across the oval to mark where the eyes will sit. This oval carries the whole hello kitty drawing, so take a moment to make it smooth and even.'
    tip: 'A wide oval, not a circle, is the secret to her look.'
  - title: Add the Ears
    body: 'At the top corners of the oval, add two small triangle ears with softly rounded tips. Space them wide apart, following the curve of the head. The ears should look like a natural continuation of the oval rather than parts stuck on top, so blend their bases into the head outline.'
    tip: 'Round the ear tips so she stays soft and cute.'
  - title: Place the Bow
    body: 'On her left ear — the right side as you look at the page — draw the bow: a round centre knot with two plump loops fanning out, tilted slightly. Let the bow overlap the ear and a little of the head. This bow is the signature of every hello kitty drawing, so make it neat and bold.'
    tip: 'Draw the centre circle first, then grow the loops from it.'
  - title: Add Eyes, Nose and Whiskers
    body: 'Place two small vertical oval eyes low on the head, wide apart and level with each other. Between and just below them, add the nose: a small horizontal oval. Then draw three whiskers on each cheek, fanning gently — the top whisker angles up, the middle runs straight, the bottom angles down. No mouth: that is part of her design.'
    tip: 'Low, wide eyes keep her face true to the character.'
  - title: Sketch the Body and Dress
    body: 'Below the head, draw a small body about half the head height: a simple dress shape that flares gently from the neck, or overalls with two straps. Keep the body plain and symmetrical — the big head is meant to dominate a hello kitty easy drawing.'
    tip: 'A trapezoid with soft corners makes an easy dress.'
  - title: Add Arms and Legs
    body: 'Add two short arms angling down from the sides of the dress, ending in simple rounded paws with no fingers. Below the dress, draw two short legs with oval shoes. Keep limbs stubby and even on both sides so she stands balanced and cute.'
    tip: 'Mirror each arm and leg against a centre line.'
  - title: Clean the Guide Lines
    body: 'Erase the eye-line guide and any construction marks inside the head and dress. Redraw the outline in one confident, even weight all the way around. Check the face once more: low eyes, yellow-nose position marked, six whiskers, bow in place — then she is ready for colour.'
    tip: 'A single clean outline weight suits this flat style best.'
  - title: Colour Hello Kitty and Finish
    body: 'Colour her head and body white (leave the paper white and outline boldly), the bow and dress pink, and the nose warm yellow. Fill the eyes and whiskers with black. Add a whisper of pink on the cheeks if you like, and your Hello Kitty drawing is finished — simple, tidy, and unmistakable.'
    tip: 'Leave her face white; colour only the bow, dress, and nose.'

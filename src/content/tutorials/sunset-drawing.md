intro: >-
  A sunset drawing is a composition problem first: horizon, sun placement, and
  value bands. This intermediate tutorial blocks the landscape or waterline,

  value structure matters more. Your finished scene should feel calm and layered
  rather than noisy. Keep the sun’s edges softer than foreground silhouettes if
  you want depth.

steps:
  - title: Place the main construction shapes
    body: >-
      Sketch the first structure with pale lines: low horizon, round sun disk,
      layered clouds, and simple silhouettes. Keep the marks loose and look at
      the whole page rather than one detail. This is the only place where the
      full sunset drawing phrase needs attention; after that, the drawing can
      grow from landmarks. Leave enough margin around the wide horizon with
      glowing sky bands so later refinements do not feel cramped.
    tip: Use the side of the pencil for soft construction lines.
  - title: Block in the horizon line
    body: >-
      Add the horizon line using simple curves that follow the first shape.
      Compare their size to the main body before adding detail. If the spacing
      feels uneven, redraw the guide rather than forcing the final outline.
      Lightly mark where the sun disk will sit so the parts relate to each other
      and the silhouette stays readable.
    tip: Check the largest spacing before erasing any guide lines.
  - title: Set the sun disk and cloud bands
    body: >-
      Place the sun disk next, then attach the cloud bands with a clean overlap.
      Watch for tangents where two edges only touch; a small overlap usually
      looks more natural. Keep the new lines lighter than the main contour. The
      goal is to show how the features connect to the form, not to finish every
      small texture mark yet.
    tip: Overlap forms clearly so each part feels attached.
  - title: Refine the outside contour
    body: >-
      Trace around the outer edge slowly and turn the basic shapes into a more
      specific contour. Use longer strokes on calm areas and shorter strokes
      where the form changes direction. Adjust the wide horizon with glowing sky
      bands before adding texture. If one side feels too heavy, compare the
      empty space around it and shave the line back with light erasing.
    tip: Darken only the contour you are sure about.
  - title: Add subject details
    body: >-
      Work on sky gradient and reflected light bands with small marks that
      follow the surface. Keep the details grouped instead of spreading
      identical marks everywhere. Add a few accents near the focal area, then
      leave quieter spaces so the drawing can breathe. The water reflection and
      silhouette should support the structure rather than distract from the main
      shape.
    tip: Cluster detail near the focal point and simplify the edges.
  - title: Clean the guide lines
    body: >-
      Erase construction lines that cut through finished features, especially
      around the horizon line and cloud bands. Do not scrub the paper; lift
      graphite slowly and redraw any softened edges afterward. This cleanup
      stage is also a good time to correct small proportion issues. Step back
      from the page and check whether the subject still reads clearly at a
      glance.
    tip: Use a kneaded eraser if the paper surface is delicate.
  - title: Add light shading and finish
    body: >-
      Choose one light direction and place gentle shadows where forms overlap or
      turn away. Add a cast shadow only if it helps ground the sunset in the
      evening landscape scene. For this sunset drawing, keep highlights open and
      avoid covering the whole sketch with gray. Finish by strengthening the
      most important contour lines and softening any leftover construction
      marks.
    tip: One consistent light source is better than many scattered shadows.
  - title: Build richer gradient texture
    body: >-
      For a fuller version, add secondary texture around the gradient and
      distant hills. Let the marks change length and pressure so they do not
      form a repeated pattern. Follow the direction of the form: curve strokes
      around round areas and angle them along flat planes. Stop before the
      texture covers the drawing's clean construction.
    tip: Vary stroke length to avoid a stamped texture.
  - title: Strengthen depth and overlap
    body: >-
      Deepen the small shadows under overlapping parts and near the base of the
      subject. The distant hills should sit behind or in front of nearby shapes
      with clear edge priority. Use slightly darker line weight where one form
      crosses another. These contrast choices make the drawing feel layered
      without adding unnecessary background detail.
    tip: Darker overlap lines create depth quickly.
  - title: Review proportions before final lines
    body: >-
      Before the final pass, compare the main height, width, and angle of the
      sketch. Look at the sky glow in relation to the full silhouette and
      correct any drift with pale marks. A stronger sunset drawing comes from
      these quiet checks. Once the structure feels balanced, redraw the final
      contour with confident pressure and keep interior lines lighter.
    tip: A final proportion check prevents heavy corrections later.

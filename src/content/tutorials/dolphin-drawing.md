intro: >-
  Dolphins need a continuous curved spine and a beak that grows naturally from
  the head. This dolphin drawing tutorial builds a flowing gesture line, then
  thickens the body and places the dorsal and flukes. Intermediate care goes
  into the melon forehead and flipper angle. Your finished dolphin should look
  buoyant, with a smile-line that does not become a cartoon grin unless you
  choose that variation. Keep the body thickest ahead of the dorsal fin.
beforeYouStart: >-
  Set the page so there is room for the full curved body with lifted flukes. Use
  an HB pencil for the first pass, keep the pressure pale, and mark the largest
  direction lines before drawing clean water-sleek shading. A small scrap sheet
  is useful for testing curves and shadows.
refine: >-

shading: >-
  Shade lightly from one direction so the beak, dorsal fin, and flippers share
  the same light source. Deepen small contact shadows and leave highlights open
  on the most forward forms.

steps:
  - title: Place the main construction shapes
    body: >-
      Sketch the first structure with pale lines: smooth body arc, rounded
      melon, beak, flippers, and flukes. Keep the marks loose and look at the
      whole page rather than one detail. This is the only place where the full
      dolphin drawing phrase needs attention; after that, the drawing can grow
      from landmarks. Leave enough margin around the curved body with lifted
      flukes so later refinements do not feel cramped.
    tip: Use the side of the pencil for soft construction lines.
  - title: Block in the beak
    body: >-
      Add the beak using simple curves that follow the first shape. Compare
      their size to the main body before adding detail. If the spacing feels
      uneven, redraw the guide rather than forcing the final outline. Lightly
      mark where the dorsal fin will sit so the parts relate to each other and
      the silhouette stays readable.
    tip: Check the largest spacing before erasing any guide lines.
  - title: Set the dorsal fin and flippers
    body: >-
      Place the dorsal fin next, then attach the flippers with a clean overlap.
      Watch for tangents where two edges only touch; a small overlap usually
      looks more natural. Keep the new lines lighter than the main contour. The
      goal is to show how the features connect to the form, not to finish every
      small texture mark yet.
    tip: Overlap forms clearly so each part feels attached.
  - title: Refine the outside contour
    body: >-
      Trace around the outer edge slowly and turn the basic shapes into a more
      specific contour. Use longer strokes on calm areas and shorter strokes
      where the form changes direction. Adjust the curved body with lifted
      flukes before adding texture. If one side feels too heavy, compare the
      empty space around it and shave the line back with light erasing.
    tip: Darken only the contour you are sure about.
  - title: Add subject details
    body: >-
      Work on clean water-sleek shading with small marks that follow the
      surface. Keep the details grouped instead of spreading identical marks
      everywhere. Add a few accents near the focal area, then leave quieter
      spaces so the drawing can breathe. The tail flukes and melon should
      support the structure rather than distract from the main shape.
    tip: Cluster detail near the focal point and simplify the edges.
  - title: Clean the guide lines
    body: >-
      Erase construction lines that cut through finished features, especially
      around the beak and flippers. Do not scrub the paper; lift graphite slowly
      and redraw any softened edges afterward. This cleanup stage is also a good
      time to correct small proportion issues. Step back from the page and check
      whether the subject still reads clearly at a glance.
    tip: Use a kneaded eraser if the paper surface is delicate.
  - title: Add light shading and finish
    body: >-
      Choose one light direction and place gentle shadows where forms overlap or
      turn away. Add a cast shadow only if it helps ground the dolphin in the
      leaping ocean pose. For this dolphin drawing, keep highlights open and
      avoid covering the whole sketch with gray. Finish by strengthening the
      most important contour lines and softening any leftover construction
      marks.
    tip: One consistent light source is better than many scattered shadows.
  - title: Build richer belly curve texture
    body: >-
      For a fuller version, add secondary texture around the belly curve and
      eye. Let the marks change length and pressure so they do not form a
      repeated pattern. Follow the direction of the form: curve strokes around
      round areas and angle them along flat planes. Stop before the texture
      covers the drawing's clean construction.
    tip: Vary stroke length to avoid a stamped texture.
  - title: Strengthen depth and overlap
    body: >-
      Deepen the small shadows under overlapping parts and near the base of the
      subject. The eye should sit behind or in front of nearby shapes with clear
      edge priority. Use slightly darker line weight where one form crosses
      another. These contrast choices make the drawing feel layered without
      adding unnecessary background detail.
    tip: Darker overlap lines create depth quickly.
  - title: Review proportions before final lines
    body: >-
      Before the final pass, compare the main height, width, and angle of the
      sketch. Look at the smile line in relation to the full silhouette and
      correct any drift with pale marks. A stronger dolphin drawing comes from
      these quiet checks. Once the structure feels balanced, redraw the final
      contour with confident pressure and keep interior lines lighter.
    tip: A final proportion check prevents heavy corrections later.

faqs:
  - question: How to draw a sun step by step?
    answer: 'Draw a circle, mark four rays at the clock points, then fill in alternating long and short rays all the way around. Add a happy face, clean the guides, and colour it golden. That is exactly how to draw a sun in this tutorial.'
  - question: How do I space the rays evenly?
    answer: 'Start with four rays pointing up, right, down, and left. Add one ray between each pair, then one more between those if you want sixteen. Working in rounds keeps the spacing even automatically.'
  - question: What is a sun and moon drawing?
    answer: 'A sun and moon drawing combines both in one design: a golden sun with rays on one side and a pale crescent moon with stars on the other, often sharing a single circle. It symbolises day and night together.'
  - question: Should the sun have a face?
    answer: 'For kids and cheerful scenes, yes — a simple smile and closed eyes make the sun friendly. For landscapes, skip the face and spend the effort on layered rays and a glowing horizon instead.'
  - question: What colours make the sun look hot?
    answer: 'Pale yellow at the centre, golden yellow on the face, orange at the ray bases, and a whisper of deeper orange at the tips. Keep the middle bright so the sun seems to shine.'

steps:
  - title: Draw the Sun's Circle
    body: 'Sketch a big, round circle in the centre of the page — this is the face of the sun. Keep it pale and leave a wide margin on every side for the rays. If freehand circles wobble, trace a plate or jar lid without shame; every sun drawing starts better with a confident circle.'
    tip: 'A template circle is a tool, not cheating.'
  - title: Add the Long Rays
    body: 'Draw eight long triangular rays around the circle, starting with the clock points — up, right, down, left — then one between each pair. Let each ray taper to a soft point. Spacing them in rounds like this is the trick that keeps a drawing of the sun even all the way around.'
    tip: 'Clock points first, then fill the gaps.'
  - title: Add the Short Rays
    body: 'Between every pair of long rays, add one short ray, about half the length. The alternating rhythm of long and short is what makes sunshine look like sunshine. Keep the short rays the same style — gentle triangles with slightly curved sides.'
    tip: 'Short rays sit exactly midway between long ones.'
  - title: Draw the Face
    body: 'Give the sun a cheerful face: two closed happy eyes as upward arcs, a wide smile, and round rosy cheeks below the eyes. Centre the face carefully — an off-centre face makes the whole sun feel dizzy. This is the step where a simple sun drawing easy becomes irresistible.'
    tip: 'Closed arc eyes are the friendliest and simplest to draw.'
  - title: Refine the Outline
    body: 'Go around the circle and rays, smoothing joins and evening out any ray that leans or crowds its neighbour. Sharpen ray tips into soft points and make sure the face lines are clean and bold. Step back: the silhouette should read as pure sunshine at a glance.'
    tip: 'Compare opposite rays — pairs should roughly match in length.'
  - title: Clean the Guide Lines
    body: 'Erase the construction circle marks that cross the rays, plus any stray spokes inside the face. Redraw softened edges. With the guides gone, the sun should look crisp, round, and ready to glow with colour.'
    tip: 'Clean inside the face first so the features stay sharp.'
  - title: Colour the Sun and Finish
    body: 'Colour the face golden yellow, the rays a deeper sunny orange at the base fading to yellow at the tips, and the cheeks a soft rose pink. Add a pale glow ring around the rays if you like. Your sun drawing is finished — warm, round, and beaming.'
    tip: 'Keep the centre of the face the palest for a glowing effect.'

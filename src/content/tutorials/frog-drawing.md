faqs:
  - question: What is the easiest way to start a frog drawing?
    answer: 'Draw a wide oval for the body and two bumps on top for the eyes. Add a big smile, folded back legs, and webbed feet. That simple scaffold makes frog drawing easy, and the face does the rest.'
  - question: How do I make my frog drawing cute?
    answer: 'Big eyes sitting high in their bumps, a very wide smile, rosy cheeks, and a round squat body. Bright spring-green colour and a lily pad underneath push a cute frog drawing over the top.'
  - question: Why do frogs have bumps on their heads?
    answer: 'Frog eyes sit on top of the head so the frog can see while mostly submerged. Drawing those two bumps correctly is the fastest way to make your frog read as a frog and not a green mouse.'
  - question: How do I draw webbed toes?
    answer: 'Draw three long toes fanning from each foot, then connect them with shallow scallop lines near the base. The webbing should reach about halfway up the toes.'
  - question: What colours do frogs come in?
    answer: 'Most drawing frogs are spring green with cream bellies, but real frogs can be brown, yellow, red, or bright blue. Keep the belly pale, whatever colour you choose for the back.'

intro: 'A frog drawing is a friendly pile of ovals: a wide body, two perky eye bumps, and folded legs with webbed toes. This frog drawing lesson builds a cute frog from those simple shapes, adds a big smile and a pale belly, and finishes in fresh pond greens. It is an easy frog drawing for beginners and kids — and cute enough to hop onto cards, stickers, and sketchbook covers.'
beforeYouStart: 'Frogs sit low and wide, so draw a pale ground line and start the body oval just above it, leaving headroom for the eye bumps. Use an HB pencil, keep shapes loose, and plan a lily pad underneath if you want a scene at the end.'
refine: 'Refine the frog by checking the eye bumps: they should match in size and sit close together on top of the head. Smooth the body into one wide curve, make the folded back legs mirror each other, and clean the construction ovals from the belly area.'
shading: 'Shade the frog greenest along the back and legs, keep the belly and throat cream, and add a soft shadow beneath the body. A few darker spots on the back and a sparkle in each eye bring the little amphibian to life.'
beginnerVariation: 'For a simple frog drawing, draw a round body, two eye bumps with dot eyes, a smile, and two feet with three toes each. Skip the leg folds and belly — the face alone makes it unmistakably a frog.'
detailedVariation: 'For a pond scene, sit the frog on a lily pad with a notched edge, add a pink water lily beside it, and draw gentle ripple rings in the water. A couple of dragonfly wings in the corner finish the story.'
conclusion: 'Wide body, eye bumps, folded legs, enormous smile — that is the whole frog. Draw the simple version a few times, then seat your frog on a lily pad and you have a pond scene worth framing.'
commonMistakes:
  - 'Placing the eyes flat on the face like a teddy bear — frog eyes sit in bumps on top of the head.'
  - 'Drawing the back legs straight instead of folded in a Z against the body.'
  - 'Forgetting webbed toes: frog feet have long toes connected by webbing.'
  - 'Making the body tall and narrow; frogs are wide, low, and squat.'
  - 'A tiny mouth — the wide frog smile should stretch almost cheek to cheek.'
tips:
  - 'Eye bumps sit close together at the top centre of the head.'
  - 'The back leg folds like a Z: thigh forward, shin back, foot forward.'
  - 'Three long toes per foot, with a hint of webbing between them.'
  - 'The smile line can stretch almost the full width of the face.'
  - 'Keep the belly big, pale, and oval — it is the frog’s charm.'

steps:
  - title: Draw the Body Oval
    body: 'Sketch a wide, squat oval low on the page — a frog body is broader than it is tall, like a soft green hill. Rest it just above a pale ground line. This oval is the whole frog in embryo, so make it generous: narrow frogs never look right.'
    tip: 'Wide and low beats tall and round for frogs.'
  - title: Add the Head and Eye Bumps
    body: 'Across the top of the oval, draw two round bumps side by side, close together at the centre — these hold the eyes. Blend the head into the body with a smooth brow curve; frogs have no neck, so the head and body are one friendly mass. The bumps give the frog drawing its unmistakable silhouette.'
    tip: 'Eye bumps touch each other at the top centre.'
  - title: Sketch the Back Legs
    body: 'On each side of the body, draw the folded back leg: a plump thigh curving out and down, folding back in at the knee like a Z. The legs should mirror each other and hug the body sides. Folded legs are what make a sitting frog look ready to hop.'
    tip: 'Think of each back leg as a soft Z shape.'
  - title: Add the Front Legs and Toes
    body: 'Draw two slim front legs dropping from under the chin to the ground, ending in small feet. Then give every foot three long toes with shallow webbing scallops between them — back feet especially. Webbed toes are a small detail that sells the whole frog.'
    tip: 'Toes fan out; webbing connects their bases.'
  - title: Draw the Eyes and Smile
    body: 'In each bump, draw a big round eye with a dark pupil and a white highlight. Then stretch a wide, gentle smile almost from cheek to cheek beneath the eyes. Add two tiny nostril dots above the smile. This happy face is the heart of a cute frog drawing, so enjoy it.'
    tip: 'The smile should be the widest line on the face.'
  - title: Add Belly and Throat
    body: 'Draw a large pale oval on the front of the body for the belly and throat patch, rising between the front legs and under the smile. Keep its top edge soft. The contrast between the green back and cream belly is what makes a frog drawing look soft and three-dimensional.'
    tip: 'The belly oval hides inside the body outline — never cross it.'
  - title: Clean the Guide Lines
    body: 'Erase construction lines where the legs cross the body and any marks inside the belly. Redraw the outer silhouette in one smooth weight. Check the frog reads at a glance: bumps, smile, folded legs, webbed toes — then it is ready for the pond colours.'
    tip: 'Keep toe webbing lines; they are detail, not guides.'
  - title: Colour the Frog and Finish
    body: 'Colour the back and legs fresh green, the belly and throat cream, and the eyes dark with white sparkles. Add rosy cheeks and a few soft spots on the back if you like. Sit the frog on a green lily pad with a pink flower, and your frog drawing is ready to hop off the page.'
    tip: 'A lily pad turns the portrait into a story.'

faqs:
  - question: What is the easiest way to start a snake drawing?
    answer: 'Draw one smooth S-shaped path line, then build the body evenly on both sides of it, thicker near the head and tapering at the tail. That single trick keeps snake drawing easy and the proportions believable.'
  - question: How do I draw a cute snake instead of a scary one?
    answer: 'Use a rounded head, big eyes with round pupils, a small smile, and rosy cheeks. Coil the body like a cinnamon roll and choose bright colours — cuteness is mostly in the face.'
  - question: How do snake belly scales work?
    answer: 'Belly scales, called scutes, run across the underside in wide bands. On a side-view snake, draw them as curved lines crossing the belly, following the body wave like rungs on a curving ladder.'
  - question: What patterns can I give my snake?
    answer: 'Bands are easiest: rings of colour around the body at intervals. Diamonds down the back look striking, and speckles are the most forgiving. Whatever you choose, bend the pattern with the body curves.'
  - question: How long should the tongue be?
    answer: 'Short and neat — about the length of the head — with a clear fork at the end. A slightly wavy tongue looks like it is flicking and tasting the air.'

intro: 'A snake drawing is really one beautiful line: a smooth S-curve that thickens into a body, narrows to a tail, and lifts into a friendly head. This snake drawing lesson starts with that wavy path, builds the body around it, and finishes with belly scales, a simple pattern, and a flicking forked tongue. It is a snake drawing easy enough for kids, with a cute snake drawing style throughout.'
beforeYouStart: 'Plan the wave first: snakes read best in a lazy S or a loose coil, so sketch the path line pale and check it fills the page gracefully. Use an HB pencil, leave room at one end for the raised head, and keep the tail end free to taper away.'
refine: 'Refine the snake by following the body from head to tail, checking the width swells gently behind the head, stays even through the middle, and tapers smoothly to the tail tip. Clean the centre path line, soften any kinks in the curves, and strengthen the outline where the body overlaps itself.'
shading: 'Shade along one side of the body to make it round, keeping a pale highlight running down the back. The belly scales stay lightest. A soft shadow beneath the curves grounds the snake and shows where it rests on the surface.'
beginnerVariation: 'For a cute snake drawing, coil the body into a simple spiral, give the head big round eyes and rosy cheeks, and use rainbow pattern bands. The cuter the face, the friendlier the snake.'
detailedVariation: 'For a snake head drawing study, fill the page with just the head: scale plates on the crown, a clear eye with a vertical pupil, nostril pits, and the forked tongue flicking out. The same construction works, drawn large.'
conclusion: 'Master the wavy path and every snake drawing becomes easy: body, head, belly, pattern. Try the coiled cute version next, then a long swimming S — the steps stay exactly the same.'
commonMistakes:
  - 'Letting the body change width suddenly, which makes the snake look like a string of sausages.'
  - 'Kinks in the curves — snake bodies bend in smooth, continuous waves.'

  - 'Drawing scale patterns that ignore the body curve, so they look pasted on.'

  - 'Draw the centre path first and build both sides around it evenly.'
  - 'The body is thickest just behind the head and tapers to the tail.'
  - 'Belly scales follow the curve like rungs on a ladder.'

steps:
  - title: Draw the Wavy Body Line
    body: 'Sketch one smooth, lazy S-curve across the page — this path is the spine of the snake drawing. Let it wave at least twice and lift slightly at the front end where the head will rise. Keep the line pale and flowing; every later step hangs on this single curve, so redraw it until it feels graceful.'
    tip: 'Move your whole arm to keep the wave smooth.'
  - title: Build the Body Curves
    body: 'Draw the two sides of the body around the path line: thickest just behind where the head will be, even through the middle waves, and tapering to a fine point at the tail. Follow the path faithfully so the body never kinks or changes width without warning.'
    tip: 'Check both sides are an even distance from the path.'
  - title: Add the Head
    body: 'At the raised end, widen the body gently into a rounded head — like a smooth thumb shape flowing out of the neck. Add a soft jaw line under the head. The head should feel like part of the body, not a separate ball glued on, so blend the curves where they meet.'
    tip: 'A snake head is only slightly wider than its neck.'
  - title: Draw the Tongue and Eye
    body: 'Give the snake a round eye with a friendly pupil, placed on the side of the head, and a small smile line. From the front of the mouth, flick out a thin forked tongue — short, wavy, and clearly split at the end. These details turn a plain snake drawing into a character.'
    tip: 'The fork is essential — draw both tips clearly.'
  - title: Add Belly Scales
    body: 'Along the underside of the body, draw a series of curved lines crossing the belly like rungs on a ladder, following each wave of the body. Space them evenly and keep them inside the outline. On raised curves, the belly shows more; on resting curves, less — let the wave decide.'
    tip: 'Belly scale lines should bend with the body, never run straight.'
  - title: Add the Pattern
    body: 'Choose a simple pattern: bands ringing the body at intervals are easiest and always look good. Draw each band as two curves that wrap around the body, bending with its direction. Alternatively, add a row of diamonds along the back. Keep the pattern off the belly scales.'
    tip: 'Bands look best spaced a little wider as the body thickens.'
  - title: Clean the Guide Lines
    body: 'Erase the centre path line and any stray construction marks, lifting graphite gently where the body overlaps. Redraw the outline in one smooth weight from head to tail. The snake should read as one continuous, curving creature before colour goes on.'
    tip: 'Follow the whole body with your eye once before colouring.'
  - title: Colour the Snake and Finish
    body: 'Colour the back a friendly green, the belly scales cream, and the pattern bands a deeper green or warm orange. Add a pink-red tongue, a dark eye with a white sparkle, and a soft shadow under the resting curves. Your snake drawing is complete — smooth, patterned, and pleasantly cute.'
    tip: 'A pale highlight down the back makes the body look round.'

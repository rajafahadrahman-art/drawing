  Sunflowers reward patience with the seed disk more than with fancy petal
  tricks. Here you will map a large circular head, divide the disk lightly, and
  attach long petals that radiate without becoming identical clones. The lesson
  is beginner level and focuses on even spacing plus a sturdy stem. Your result
  should look sunny and upright, with a textured center suggestion and petals
  that taper cleanly. Measure petal length against the disk diameter before you
  darken anything.

steps:
  - title: Place the main construction shapes
    body: >-
      Sketch the first structure with pale lines: large circular seed disk, long
      ray petals, and a sturdy stalk. Keep the marks loose and look at the whole
      page rather than one detail. This is the only place where the full
      sunflower drawing phrase needs attention; after that, the drawing can grow
      from landmarks. Leave enough margin around the wide flower head with a
      tall vertical stem so later refinements do not feel cramped.
    tip: Use the side of the pencil for soft construction lines.
  - title: Block in the seed disk
    body: >-
      Add the seed disk using simple curves that follow the first shape. Compare
      their size to the main body before adding detail. If the spacing feels
      uneven, redraw the guide rather than forcing the final outline. Lightly
      mark where the ray petals will sit so the parts relate to each other and
      the silhouette stays readable.
    tip: Check the largest spacing before erasing any guide lines.
  - title: Set the ray petals and stalk
    body: >-
      Place the ray petals next, then attach the stalk with a clean overlap.
      Watch for tangents where two edges only touch; a small overlap usually
      looks more natural. Keep the new lines lighter than the main contour. The
      goal is to show how the features connect to the form, not to finish every
      small texture mark yet.
    tip: Overlap forms clearly so each part feels attached.
  - title: Refine the outside contour
    body: >-
      Trace around the outer edge slowly and turn the basic shapes into a more
      specific contour. Use longer strokes on calm areas and shorter strokes
      where the form changes direction. Adjust the wide flower head with a tall
      vertical stem before adding texture. If one side feels too heavy, compare
      the empty space around it and shave the line back with light erasing.
    tip: Darken only the contour you are sure about.
  - title: Add subject details
    body: >-
      Work on seed dots and broad leaf veins with small marks that follow the
      surface. Keep the details grouped instead of spreading identical marks
      everywhere. Add a few accents near the focal area, then leave quieter
      spaces so the drawing can breathe. The broad leaves and petal tips should
      support the structure rather than distract from the main shape.
    tip: Cluster detail near the focal point and simplify the edges.
  - title: Clean the guide lines
    body: >-
      Erase construction lines that cut through finished features, especially
      around the seed disk and stalk. Do not scrub the paper; lift graphite
      slowly and redraw any softened edges afterward. This cleanup stage is also
      a good time to correct small proportion issues. Step back from the page
      and check whether the subject still reads clearly at a glance.
    tip: Use a kneaded eraser if the paper surface is delicate.
  - title: Add light shading and finish
    body: >-
      Choose one light direction and place gentle shadows where forms overlap or
      turn away. Add a cast shadow only if it helps ground the sunflower in the
      summer garden study. For this sunflower drawing, keep highlights open and
      avoid covering the whole sketch with gray. Finish by strengthening the
      most important contour lines and softening any leftover construction
      marks.
    tip: One consistent light source is better than many scattered shadows.

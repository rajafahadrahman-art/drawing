faqs:
  - question: What is the easiest way to start a fire drawing?
    answer: 'Start with two crossed logs, then draw one big teardrop flame above them. Add a smaller flame inside, clean the guide lines, and colour from a pale yellow centre out to orange edges. That simple order makes fire drawing easy for any beginner.'
  - question: How do I make my drawing of fire look real and not flat?
    answer: 'Use curved, tapering flame tongues instead of straight triangles, lean them all slightly in one direction, and keep the centre pale while the edges go deeper orange and red. A soft glow behind the flames also helps.'
  - question: What colours should I use for flames?
    answer: 'Work from the inside out: white or pale yellow at the centre, then yellow, orange, and a touch of red at the outer edges and tips. Logs look best in warm browns with darker cracks.'

h1: 'How to Draw Fire: Fire Drawing Tutorial'
intro: 'A fire drawing is really three drawings in one: a steady log base, a tall outer flame, and smaller flame tongues dancing inside it. This fire drawing lesson builds the campfire from simple shapes so the flames sit naturally on the logs, then finishes with sparks, embers, and a warm glow. It is a fire drawing easy enough for complete beginners, and quick to practise again and again.'
beforeYouStart: 'Set the page tall rather than wide, because flames grow upward. Use an HB pencil for the first pass, keep the pressure pale, and leave extra space above the logs so the tallest flame tongue has room to flicker. A scrap sheet is useful for testing wavy flame curves before you commit to them.'
refine: 'Refine the fire by comparing the outer flame silhouette against the inner tongues. Flames should taper to soft points and lean in one gentle direction, like a real campfire in light wind. Clean the construction lines that cross the logs, then strengthen only the edges that separate one flame layer from the next.'
shading: 'Fire glows from the inside out, so keep the centre of the flame palest and deepen the colour or shading toward the outer edges. Add a soft glow halo behind the flames and small dark shadows between the logs so the campfire feels grounded and warm.'
beginnerVariation: 'For a fire drawing easy version, draw one big flame, one inner flame, and two crossed logs. Skip the sparks and smoke, use one clean outline, and colour the flame with a single orange and a yellow centre.'
detailedVariation: 'For a more detailed drawing of fire, add a second row of logs, rising smoke curls, and heat wave lines above the tips. Layer three or four flame tongues, vary their heights, and scatter embers at different sizes so the fire feels alive.'
conclusion: 'Keep the finished fire simple: strong logs, one bold outer flame, and a few inner tongues. Once this version feels easy, try the drawing again with taller flames or a gentle wind lean, and compare how the mood changes.'
commonMistakes:
  - 'Making every flame the same height, which makes the fire look like a row of candles instead of one campfire.'
  - 'Drawing flames as stiff triangles with straight sides instead of soft, curving tongues.'
  - 'Floating the flames above the logs with a gap, so the fire never looks connected to its fuel.'
  - 'Adding sparks and embers before the main flame shapes read clearly.'
  - 'Colouring the whole flame one flat orange instead of keeping a pale, hot centre.'
tips:
  - 'Draw flame curves in one confident stroke, moving your whole arm rather than your wrist.'
  - 'Let the tallest flame lean slightly, then lean the smaller tongues the same way.'
  - 'Keep the inner flame roughly half the height of the outer flame.'
  - 'Cross the logs in an X so the fire has a stable, believable base.'
  - 'Leave the very centre of the flame white or pale yellow for the hottest glow.'
  - 'Add sparks last, and make them smaller as they rise.'
steps:
  - title: Draw the Log Base
    body: 'Sketch two logs crossing in an X at the bottom of the page, with a third short log resting on top. Keep the lines pale and simple: long rounded rectangles with small circles at the cut ends. This base anchors the whole fire drawing, so check that it sits level and leaves plenty of room above for tall flames.'
    tip: 'Draw the cut ends of the logs as simple ovals.'
  - title: Sketch the Outer Flame Shape
    body: 'Above the logs, draw one large flame: a wide teardrop that rises to a soft, flickering point. Let the sides curve gently inward as they climb, and allow the tip to lean slightly to one side. This outer shape is the silhouette of your drawing of fire, so take your time getting its height and lean to feel natural.'
    tip: 'Flames are widest near the logs and narrowest at the tip.'
  - title: Add the Inner Flames
    body: 'Inside the big flame, draw a second, smaller flame in the same style, roughly half the height. Offset it slightly toward the lean so the two shapes feel like one moving fire. The gap between the two outlines becomes the middle band of colour later, so keep it even and clean.'
    tip: 'Inner flames echo the outer shape, only smaller.'
  - title: Draw the Middle Flame Tongues
    body: 'Add two or three separate flame tongues around the main flame: smaller teardrops that rise from the logs at the sides and flick between the big shapes. Vary their heights — no two tongues should match exactly. These tongues are what make drawing fire feel alive instead of stamped out.'
    tip: 'Odd numbers of tongues look more natural than even ones.'
  - title: Refine the Flame Outline
    body: 'Trace the flame outlines slowly, softening any stiff corners into smooth curves and sharpening the tips into gentle points. Deepen the curves where flames curl, and erase any double lines. Compare the silhouette against real campfire photos in your mind: the edges should wave, never run straight for long.'
    tip: 'Turn the paper upside down to spot stiff, unnatural curves.'
  - title: Add Sparks and Embers
    body: 'Scatter small sparks above the flames: tiny dots and short dashes that shrink as they rise. Add a few round embers nestled between the logs at the base. Keep the sparks light and sparse — a dozen is plenty — so they suggest heat without cluttering the fire drawing.'
    tip: 'Place the biggest embers low, between the logs.'
  - title: Clean the Guide Lines
    body: 'Erase construction lines that cross the flames or logs, lifting the graphite slowly so you do not smudge the curves. Redraw any softened edges with a clean stroke. Step back and check the fire reads clearly at a glance: bold flames, steady logs, and a little sparkle of embers.'
    tip: 'A kneaded eraser lifts lines without tearing the paper.'
  - title: Colour the Fire and Finish
    body: 'Colour from the centre out: pale yellow in the middle of the flame, yellow around it, orange on the outer band, and a whisper of red at the tips. Colour the logs warm brown with darker cracks, add a soft glow behind the flames, and your fire drawing is finished.'
    tip: 'Leave a tiny white highlight in the centre for the hottest glow.'

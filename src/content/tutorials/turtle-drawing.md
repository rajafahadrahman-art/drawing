faqs:
  - question: How to draw a turtle step by step?
    answer: 'Draw a wide dome for the shell, add a rim and belly line, attach a round head at the front, then two big front flippers and two smaller back ones. Finish with shell plates, a face, and colour. Those steps are exactly how to draw a turtle in this tutorial.'
  - question: What is the difference between a turtle drawing and a sea turtle drawing?
    answer: 'A sea turtle drawing has flippers instead of legs, a smoother teardrop shell, and often water details like bubbles. Land turtles and tortoises have stubby legs and a higher, bumpier dome.'
  - question: How do I draw the shell pattern?
    answer: 'Draw a centre row of three or four hexagon-ish plates down the dome, then border them with smaller plates that follow the curve. Keep the shapes slightly irregular — real scutes, the shell plates, are never perfect tiles.'
  - question: Why does my turtle look like a mushroom?
    answer: 'The dome is probably too tall and the body missing. Widen the shell, lower the dome, and make sure the belly line and flippers show beneath the rim so it reads as a swimming animal.'
  - question: What colours are sea turtles?
    answer: 'Olive and green shells with tan or amber plates, lighter green skin with darker speckles, and a pale belly. The water around them can be any blue you love.'

intro: 'A turtle drawing is built around one glorious dome: the shell. This turtle drawing lesson draws a friendly sea turtle — big patterned shell, paddle flippers, and a gentle head — from simple ovals and arcs, which makes it a sea turtle drawing easy enough for first-time artists. If you have ever wondered how to draw a turtle that actually looks like it is swimming, these are the steps.'
beforeYouStart: 'Draw wide rather than tall: a swimming turtle fills the page horizontally. Use an HB pencil, sketch the shell dome big and pale in the centre, and leave room on the left for the head and around the shell for four flippers.'
refine: 'Refine the turtle by smoothing the dome into one confident curve and checking the flippers: front flippers are larger than back ones, and all four angle backward like paddles. Clean the construction ovals from under the shell rim and strengthen the shell outline.'
shading: 'Shade the shell plates a touch darker at their edges so the pattern looks tiled, keep the belly line pale, and soften a blue-green wash of shadow beneath the flippers. A few small bubbles near the head finish the underwater feeling.'
beginnerVariation: 'For a turtle drawing easy version, draw the shell as a simple dome with a smiley head and four oval flippers. Skip the plate pattern or use three big plates, and colour the shell one happy green.'
detailedVariation: 'For a detailed sea turtle, divide the shell into a full map of scutes — a centre row of hexagons bordered by smaller plates — add speckles on the flippers and head, and draw soft water lines flowing past the shell.'
conclusion: 'With a strong dome and paddle flippers, any turtle drawing can swim. Practise the easy version until the shell feels automatic, then spend your time on the plate pattern — that is where sea turtles get their beauty.'
commonMistakes:
  - 'Drawing the shell as a flat semicircle glued to a body, instead of a dome the body tucks beneath.'
  - 'Making all four flippers the same size; front flippers are the big paddles.'
  - 'Forgetting the shell rim, the band that separates the dome from the belly.'
  - 'Placing the head on top of the shell instead of extending from its front edge.'
  - 'Drawing shell plates as a perfect grid — real scutes are irregular, hand-drawn shapes.'
tips:
  - 'The dome should be about twice as wide as it is tall.'
  - 'Front flippers reach forward, back flippers angle behind — like a swimmer mid-stroke.'
  - 'Irregular hexagon plates look more natural than perfect tiles.'
  - 'A tiny tail peeks out at the back of the shell.'
  - 'Keep the eye and smile soft — sea turtles are gentle creatures.'

steps:
  - title: Draw the Shell Dome
    body: 'Sketch a wide, smooth dome in the centre of the page — like a hill twice as wide as it is tall. This dome is the carapace, the top of the shell, and the anchor of the whole turtle drawing. Keep the curve pale and confident; a wobbly dome is easy to smooth in the refine step.'
    tip: 'One long, slow stroke makes the smoothest dome.'
  - title: Add the Shell Rim and Belly Line
    body: 'Under the dome, draw a band following its curve — the shell rim — then a flatter line beneath it for the belly, the plastron. Connect the ends so the shell reads as a solid form the body tucks beneath. This rim is the detail beginners skip, and it makes the biggest difference.'
    tip: 'The rim band should be the same width all the way around.'
  - title: Sketch the Head
    body: 'At the front edge of the shell, draw a round head extending outward, slightly overlapping the rim. Sea turtle heads do not retract, so let it sit proudly outside the shell. Keep it smooth and simple for now — the face arrives in a later step.'
    tip: 'The head is about the size of a small orange next to the dome.'
  - title: Add the Flippers
    body: 'Draw two large front flippers reaching forward and down from under the rim, shaped like paddles. Add two smaller back flippers at the rear, angled behind the shell, plus a tiny tail point. Front flippers do the swimming, so make them clearly the biggest limbs in the turtle drawing.'
    tip: 'Angle all flippers backward like a swimmer mid-stroke.'
  - title: Draw the Shell Pattern
    body: 'Divide the dome into scutes: a centre row of three or four hexagon-style plates, bordered by a ring of smaller plates that follow the curve. Draw them slightly irregular on purpose. This pattern is the signature beauty of a sea turtle drawing, so take your time and keep the lines inside the dome.'
    tip: 'Irregular plates look natural; perfect tiles look printed.'
  - title: Add Face Details
    body: 'Give the turtle a round eye with a highlight, a gentle smile, and a few speckles on the head and front flippers. Add short crease lines where the flippers meet the shell. A few small bubbles floating up beside the head place the turtle happily underwater.'
    tip: 'Speckles cluster on the head and flippers, not the shell plates.'
  - title: Clean the Guide Lines
    body: 'Erase the construction dome where it doubles the rim, and lift stray marks from the belly area. Redraw the outer silhouette in one even weight. The turtle should read instantly — dome, head, paddles — before any colour touches it.'
    tip: 'Keep the belly line light; it is a subtle detail.'
  - title: Colour the Sea Turtle and Finish
    body: 'Colour the shell plates olive green with tan centres, the rim a warm amber, and the head and flippers lighter green with darker speckles. Add a soft blue wash or a few blue bubbles around the turtle, and your sea turtle drawing is swimming off the page.'
    tip: 'Warm shell colours against blue water make the turtle glow.'
